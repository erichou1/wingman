# Wingman, two-minute pitch

Deck: `docs/pitch-deck.html` (present in a browser, arrow keys) and `docs/Wingman-pitch.pdf`.
Seven slides, about 120 seconds. Say the line, then move. Do not read the slide out loud.

## The one thing to get right

The judges will remember exactly one sentence. Make it **"everyone else sorts profiles, we rehearse the conversation."** Say it on slide 4 and again on slide 7.

## If asked about the benchmark numbers

Say it straight: those are projected, not measured. The benchmark named on the chart is CRRS on the Libimseti dating split, which is the standard public reciprocal-matching set, and it's what we'd run against next. Do not claim you ran it. "Not yet, those are targets" is a completely normal hackathon answer and trying to bluff it is the only way this hurts you.

## Ad concepts, if the video isn't shot yet

Keep it under 30 seconds, one joke, no dialogue explaining the product.

**A. The double date.** Two people sit across from each other at dinner in total awkward silence. Cut to their phones on the table, propped up, facing each other. The phones are having an animated, delightful conversation. Voiceover: "Wingman. Somebody at this table should be having a good time." Cut to logo.

**B. The reference check.** Shot like a job interview. A person sits nervously while an AI voice off-screen asks their agent questions about them. "And how would you describe his relationship with dishes?" Long pause. The agent: "Aspirational." Card: "Your agent tells the truth. That's the point."

**C. The group chat.** Someone screenshots a match and sends it to their friends for a verdict. Twelve replies, all contradictory, no one agrees. Cut to Wingman doing it in four turns, with sources. Card: "Like asking your friends, except it read the whole thread."

A is the most shootable with two people, two phones, and one table. B is funniest if your delivery is deadpan. C needs the most editing.
